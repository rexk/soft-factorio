Anyone can see whether the factory works better than before, and say what the numbers mean

Key: metricsDate: 2026-11-06Author: an agent, with the maintainerStatus: product brief, awaiting approvalSource: the maintainer's words and answers in chat; research.md

The factory keeps a few rough numbers for each version and shows them next to earlier versions. People and agents read them and record what they think they mean. That gives us evidence, not only a feeling, that a change made things better or worse.

Value

V0. The factory's maintainer, the agents that work on it, and the authors of repositories that use it can see whether it is working better or worse than before. They get numbers that are compared over time, and they decide for themselves what the numbers mean.

  • V1. Evidence, not only a feeling: a change to a skill, rule or guide can be checked against the numbers from the version before it.
  • V2. Rough numbers are fine. A number that is easy to get, even an ambiguous one, earns its place by being comparable over time, not by being precise.
  • V3. The meaning stays with the reader. The numbers carry no verdict, and each person or agent writes their own reading of them.
  • V4. How it felt sits next to what was measured, so we can see when the two disagree.
  • V5. Safe to adopt: a repository's numbers are kept in that repository and sent nowhere else.

Behaviour

Record

IDBehaviour
B1After an eval pass, its numbers (such as how often each scenario passed, what it cost, how long it took) are kept with the factory version they measured, and the numbers from earlier passes are still there.Covers every scenario and every arm that ran. A pass on a change that hasn't landed yet is kept too, so a change can be checked before it lands.
B2In a repository that uses the factory, anyone can get that repository's numbers (such as how its agents' pull requests fared, and how often people had to correct the agent) for each factory version it has had. The numbers come from what the repository already holds, such as its history and pull requests, so nothing needs switching on beforehand.
B3Every number shows what it counts, where it came from, and how many runs or tasks it rests on, so a reader can judge how much to trust it.
B4When an agent's task ends, the person who asked for it can give a quick answer on how it went. The answer is kept next to that task's numbers. Skipping it changes nothing else.

Compare

IDBehaviour
B5Anyone can see one version's numbers next to earlier versions' numbers, with the change between them shown.For the factory, a version is a state of the factory, released or not. For a repository, it is a factory version the repository has applied.
B6A number that a version doesn't have shows as missing, never as zero. That includes a kind of number added later: older versions show it as missing, and their other numbers still compare.

Interpret

IDBehaviour
B7The numbers come with no verdict: no "good" or "bad", no overall score, no pass mark of their own.
B8A person or an agent can write down their reading of a comparison. It is kept with the numbers it refers to, along with who wrote it and when. Several readings can sit side by side, and a new reading never replaces an earlier one.The maintainer, agents, and the authors of each adopting repository can all add readings.
B9An agent can read the numbers and the earlier readings as plain, machine-readable data.

Throughout

IDBehaviour
B10Numbers, quick answers and readings about a repository stay in that repository. Nothing is sent to the factory or anywhere else.

Which numbers to start with, the order to build in (the factory's own evals first), and every question of how all belong in the design brief.
